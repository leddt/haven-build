# Level 9

Hail of Spears is one capstone for the banner build. Thematically, you are throwing each banner at a different enemy. You get one target for free and then another target for each banner you’ve been lugging around. Then, you throw the banners at the targets. It’s worth noting that you don’t have to assign each banner to a different target, you can put them all next to one target. This can be a lot of attack all at once. A dedicated Bannerspear will probably try to have two banners for the majority of the scenario, so this would be a nonloss attack 15 \- *very* respectable, even at such a poor initiative. The obvious catch is that the banners are now likely in danger. You will probably want to use this card slowly, to ensure that you have time to rescue them before the enemies counteract. Alternatively, you could pair it with granted movement to shuffle the banners backwards immediately. Overall, if you are setting up a few banners anyway and you are confident that you’ll be able to keep them safe, this action is exceptionally good. 

The bottom action validates banners in a different way. After a sizable move, you get to pull two banners from your loss pile and bring them back to life. There are two main uses for this. The first is to revive banners that have died to damage. You can plan to use the top for the majority of the scenario, and then use the bottom if AOE attacks or unlucky positioning means your banners fall. The existence of this ability lets you play much more dangerously with your banner’s HP, as one unlucky crit won’t end your entire scenario. Alternatively, you can plan ahead to make this card your main source of banners. Before you’ve rested twice, don’t run any banner actions, but lose a banner to your long rest. Then, on the third cycle, play this card and bring both banners back. You lose the banners for your first two cycles, but you get to play two banners for the price of one. It’s a very efficient way to maintain your longevity while still capitalizing on banners. Of course, the tradeoffs here are (1) that the card will be dead in your hand (at garbage initiative) on the first cycle, where you have no banners to throw; and (2) that you won’t have any banners out for the eleven turns of your first two rest cycles (including your long rests, where banners would still be putting in some work). I think the most efficient way to use this is plan for a happy medium: play a banner (or two) on the first rest cycle, lost a banner (or two) to resting, then if any of your banners die you can use this to bring back a dead banner and a lost banner. 

Take No Prisoners is very impressive\-looking. In theory this represents nonloss 27 attack with poison, but of course you’re never going to fill up every hex. Realistically, you are probably picking up somewhere in the range of 3\-6 enemies, depending on scenario and player count. That’s still a very impressive amount of attack and poison. Getting two allies directly behind you shouldn’t be that big of a deal, and it doesn’t put them in danger, so you should have no problems enabling this formation in most parties. It’s an immensely satisfying pattern that you should have no issues finding value from every cycle. 

The bottom action is a simple, powerful banner. It deals one direct damage to all enemies within a respectable range of four. This is strongest against swarms of weak, shielded enemies, and in large player counts where the number of targets is likely to be high. It also has a strong health pool, which means it is very unlikely to die by mistake. It’s not really a banner that you need to plan your build or your team around, and you don’t need to position it very precisely. You can drop it in most any scenario and it’ll justify itself with massive amounts of damage over time as long as you can grant it enough movement to keep up.
