Head of the Hammer provides a formation on a loss. Bannerspear math says you get 2XP from losses and 1XP from formations, so the impressive\-looking 3XP is par for the course. It’s one of our easiest formations to set up, just requiring an ally behind us (like [Tip of the Spear](#tip-of-the-spear)), which makes it combo very easily with the [Reinforcement](#at-all-costs) or any of our granted movement options. However, the fact that it stuns makes it a bit more valuable, since at least one of the enemies in question won’t be an immediate threat. The initiative is reliably slow here, which is not as bad for a stun as for a disarm, but still not as good as a fast initiative for this kind of action. 

The bottom gives us another move four option, with a bonus of muddle and wind. The wind may or not find value but the muddle probably will. This action is great to pair with our riskier formations: move in slowly and muddle everything, then smack. Next round, move quickly and hit one more time, then get out. You will have to coordinate with an ally to line it up properly, but it is well worth the effort. If nothing else, this gives us a reliable large movement with a strong initiative that we’re happy to take to level nine. 

Let Them Come provides a more traditional formation. Anyone who has played tinkerer knows that triangles show up all the time, so it won’t be very difficult to find a clump to maximize the pattern on. It may or may not be easy to get yourself and an ally into position, but it isn’t usually risky to do so. Attacking for two with poison is very strong, and the fact that you heal the ally means they’ll be much more excited to line up for you. Do note that you need to attack at least one enemy in order to get the healing. You can also use this card to handle enemies with standard retaliate, by ignoring the closest hex and hitting two enemies from two hexes away. Overall, this formation is fairly impressive and reliable. 

The bottom banner is a lot tankier than our level one banners. It has to be, since it needs to be so close to the action in order to be productive. It’s very difficult to get this bonus to your whole team, unlike the healing and damage we’re used to, so your best bet here is to walk it around next to the tankiest character on your team. Spoiler alert: that’s probably you. If all you do in the scenario is set up this, set up [Unbreakable Wall](#unbreakable-wall), and then heal and tank, your allies will *love* you. One shield isn’t enough to simply live but two shield is. Just be careful: since this banner needs to be directly adjacent to you, you may find yourself with a monster adjacent to both you and the banner. If that’s the case, then the monster will focus your banner given the choice. Be sure not to let that happen too frequently. If that isn’t your plan, then this banner simply reads “get 2XP when the scenario is over.” The top is exciting enough for people that want it that we are perfectly happy to pretend the bottom is blank. 

[Tactician](#tactician/summary): Let Them Come has a top action that’s perfect for us. It’s one of the easiest formations to pull off, and it’s a respectable power level on a decent initiative. 

[Stonewall](#stonewall/summary): We would really love the bottom of Head of the Hammer \- the muddle is fantastic for us, and it’s likely we aren’t entirely wasting the wind. However, the core of our build is the Banner of Courage, so it’s essential to take Let Them Come. There just isn’t time to come back for the muddle, so we’ll sadly leave it here. 

[Breezing Banner](#breezing-banner/summary): We’re taking Head of the Hammer primarily for the strong move that generates wind. Now that we have this, we won’t have to rely so heavily on Javelin to pull the weight of the wind.
