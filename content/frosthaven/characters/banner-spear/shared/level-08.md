We’ve seen a lot of actions the last few levels that are improvements over cards we’ve seen before, and at first glance it looks like Sweeping Aid is continuing the trend. The pattern and immobilize are identical to [Resolved Courage](#resolved-courage). The initiative is slow instead of fast, which is typically worse for CC and for formations, but we trade it for an extra damage and two pierce. However, there’s a very important distinction: you and the ally have swapped places, meaning it’s the ally that’s in danger instead of you. We usually prefer to be the one in danger, because we’re fairly tanky, but if the ally in question is expendable (like a falcon or a skeleton, for instance) then suddenly nobody important is adjacent to an immobilized enemy. Plus, it means we’re attacking from a distance, allowing us to ignore retaliate. That being said, if we don’t have a disposable ally then it’s likely worse to put someone squishy in danger rather than ourself. 

The bottom action is very strong. We were happy to have one healing (with regenerate) on a move three from [Meat Grinder](#level-02); now, we have four. In particular, we have four healing that we can split up between two targets as needed, or combine all on one. The fact that it is split makes it much better for dealing with conditions in particular. 

Taunting Howl has a familiar looking bottom action. It’s the same as [Combined Effort](#combined-effort), except that most of the 2s are now 3s. That’s 50% more movement and 50% more range to grant that movement. The only thing this card needs to be really busted is 50% more targets, so you’ll still have to get by with two. Still, this amount of movement means you should have no difficulty lining up any of your formations as long as you have willing allies. Even [Tri\-Thrust](#level-07) should be doable a good chunk of the time with no preparation \- and at 11 initiative, it’s something you’re going to be very happy to do. It also easily lets you bring two banners along over very reasonable distances. It’s a simple, solid card that enables much of what you’re doing otherwise. 

The top action might as well say “your team lives this round.” You pull in what usually amounts to the entire room, muddle them, and then become invincible. If you’re running this, the three shield is probably stacked on at least 1\-2 more, so you should live through most any attack. The muddle doubles down on this, ensuring that you won’t be critically hit barring some *very* bad luck. And because it’s a top action, you can plan to use it when busting into a new room and bail out if it looks like there aren’t enough dangerous targets. It’s a very strong loss if you know your team can follow up by making those enemies dead, and *especially* if clumping the enemies up first makes that outcome more likely. 

[Tactician](#tactician/summary): Both actions are *really* good for us, and we can’t come back for the other one at level nine, so we’re going to have to make a choice here. The final result is going to depend on how much difficulty you’ve had in setting up formations. If you feel like you could use some additional help, Taunting Howl’s bottom action will make positioning requirements almost trivial. If you feel like you’re usually able to pull off what you’re looking for, we can pick up Sweeping Aid. The choice is tilted further towards Sweeping Aid if your allies are making disposable summons that mean the immobilize is very likely to pay strong dividends, or if you’re running alongside a second dedicated tank who doesn’t mind standing up close and tanking the hits. 

[Stonewall](#stonewall/summary): We want both actions, and as is tradition with this build, we’ll be taking both of them, so we just have to pick one for right now. Most of the time, it’s going to be Sweeping Aid. This is an epic amount of healing that can be used in a very versitile way. However, the additional movement on Taunting Howl may end up being important, especially if you’ve decided to run two banners. 

[Breezing Banner](#breezing-banner/summary): We’re going to go back here for [Tri\-Thrust](#level-07). That’s not to say either of these actions are bad \- we can easily use both of them for their bottom action to great effect. However, we really want one more outlet for our wind, and [Tri\-Thrust](#level-07) gives us that outlet. If you know that the frontline is so crowded that you won’t be getting off these attacks frequently enough, then you can take either of the level eight cards instead, depending on whether you think your team needs healing or granted movement.
